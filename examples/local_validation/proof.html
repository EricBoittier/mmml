Dimer PES validation against a computed ab-initio reference — all 15 pairs

Reference: PBE0-D3BJ / def2-TZVP, counterpoise-corrected, computed on the identical scan geometries (1,387 points, 15 pairs) with run_gpu4pyscf_scan.py on GPU. Model: SpookyNet + fixed ZBL + CGenFF LJ, 6 Å neural cutoff, float64, CUDA. Eint = EAB − EA − EB.

Grid validated. The computed reference reproduces the literature minima on these exact geometries: DCM–DCM −2.48 @ 4.25 Å (lit. −2.44), DCM–TIP3 −2.50 (lit. −2.38), TIP3–TIP3 −2.79. Disagreements below are the model's.

The model splits cleanly by interaction type, not by element. Hydrogen-bonded / electrostatics-dominated pairs are excellent (TIP3–TIP3 0.2, TIP3–MEOH 0.4, BENZ–TIP3 0.9, MEOH–MEOH 0.9). Dispersion-dominated pairs fail badly — aromatic stacking (BENZ–BENZ, BENZ–MEOH, ACE–BENZ) and halogenated contacts (DCM–DCM, DCM–MEOH, DCM–ACE) are wrong by 5–13 kcal/mol. The model overbinds nearly all of them, except DCM–DCM, which it makes spuriously repulsive (+18 where the reference is −2.48).
Correction to an earlier hypothesis. I first attributed the failure to chlorine being nearly absent from the training cache (317 Cl atoms / 168 structures out of 282,090 — still true). But the 15-pair reference refutes it as the primary cause: Cl-containing pairs are only ~1.4× worse than Cl-free ones, and benzene pairs — whose elements are abundantly represented — fail just as hard. Sparse Cl data is a contributing factor, not the explanation.

Training degrades the surface; MBD partially recovers it

Mean binding-region RMSE: s2000 4.13 < s5000 4.41 < MBD s10000 4.71 < s10000 5.61. Continued training makes the PES steadily worse across most pairs. Adding MBD dispersion (resumed from step5000 → 10000) recovers much of that degradation (5.61 → 4.71) but still does not beat step2000. At the DCM–DCM well MBD supplies genuine dispersion (−1.19 kcal/mol), yet the neural term grows more repulsive to compensate (+15.9 → +17.6): because training targets a total energy, an added physics term is absorbed by the neural part instead of correcting it.

The degradation was a learning-rate artifact, not divergence. The same 3000 steps from step2000 at LR 1e-6 improve the surface (4.41 → 3.92) where LR 1e-5 wrecked it. The model was being over-driven.
MBD fixes dispersion exactly where predicted — but only when the model can adapt to it. At LR 1e-5 it collapses BENZ–BENZ error 8.13 → 2.22 and BENZ–MEOH 8.72 → 3.93 (the aromatic-stacking pairs). At LR 1e-6 the model cannot retrain around the new term and the benefit shrinks (BENZ–BENZ 4.04). Hence the two knobs do not combine.
What no knob fixed. ACE–BENZ (~10–12), DCM–MEOH (~7–8) and ACE–ACE (~5–11) stay broken in every arm. Overall gains are modest (4.13 → 3.92, ~5%). The dispersion representation is still the outstanding structural problem.

Numerical integrity (all pass)

ZBL exactly 0.000e+00 kcal/mol at every accepted grid point · component reconstruction ≤ 2.4e-14 eV · CGenFF LJ consumed 1305/1387 · JAX x64 + MMML_ML_DTYPE=float64 · CUDA on every run, no CPU fallback.

Verdict

No checkpoint is ready for promotion. step2000 is the best of those tested, and the model is trustworthy today only for hydrogen-bonded chemistry (water/methanol: RMSE ≈ 0.2–0.9 kcal/mol). The defect is in how the model represents dispersion-dominated interactions, and continued training on the current objective actively makes it worse.
